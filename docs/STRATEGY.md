# DreamBid: the company after the demo

September 10, 2026. This is a commercial hypothesis and operating plan, not validated demand, booked revenue, or a forecast.

## Decision

Start with **contractor-first sales and pre-construction for outdoor-living design/build firms**, using the homeowner experience as their proposal and intake surface. Keep today's backyard demo focused. Sell faster qualified-lead conversion and fewer missing-scope surprises; do not launch a two-sided marketplace yet.

Initial customer profile to test: owner or sales/pre-construction lead at a small established outdoor-living company, handling roughly 10–30 qualified opportunities a month for patio, shade, landscape lighting, and outdoor-kitchen projects. The opportunity and project-volume bands are targeting assumptions. Begin in one accessible geography—northern New Jersey / NYC suburbs—for discovery and hands-on pilots. If winter sales cycles constrain adoption, test one warm-weather partner market rather than expanding features.

The painful workflow: a homeowner sends scattered inspiration, an address and a budget; someone repeatedly visits, sketches, revises, prices and reconciles exclusions before knowing whether the customer will buy. The buyer already owns lead flow and can measure staff time, proposal turnaround, close rate and gross profit.

## Why contractor-first

| Dimension | Homeowner-first marketplace | Contractor-first platform |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency | Infrequent projects per household | Repeated incoming opportunities |
| Distribution | Acquire each household and supply-side coverage | Sell to a firm with existing leads |
| Initial payment | Design/pre-construction fee per project | Recurring team subscription with usage limits |
| Cold-start risk | Must build trust and supply on both sides | Useful to one contractor on day one |
| Conflict | Platform referral fees can distort ranking | Contractor-branded proposals must disclose whose interests they serve |
| Expansion | Transaction coordination and homeowner agent | Cost catalogs, supplier/subcontractor comparison, approved scope, change orders |
| Near-term conclusion | Keep as a future distribution/product option | Stronger hypothesis for reaching repeatable revenue by December |

The prototype's three competing contractor bids are a homeowner-facing demonstration. In a contractor-paid product, reuse the same comparison engine for subcontractor/vendor bids and completeness checks on the contractor's own proposal. Never sell a secretly pay-to-rank homeowner recommendation.

## Competitive reality

JobTread publicly lists a $199/month base including the first internal user, with the next nine users at $20 each, and offers estimating, cost catalogs, bids, and job-management features. DreamBid's proposed $99 entry and $199 core plan fit that buying context. The $399 tier needs evidence that its added project volume and shared workflow justify the price; DreamBid does not have JobTread's breadth. Houzz Pro already advertises estimates, 3D planning, photoreal renders, bid management, and AI features. The defensible hypothesis is the connection among evidence, design constraints, versioned scope and proposal outcomes—not generic AI imagery or an unqualified novelty claim. ## Competitive launch pricing

Proposed monthly USD pricing, with no setup fee or annual commitment. This replaces the earlier $2,000 subscription assumption. Plans, seats and billing are a post-hackathon offer to validate, not implemented subscription features or accepted customer contracts.

| Plan | Monthly price | Intended team | Included project packages / month |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Solo | **$99** | Owner-operator; 1 user | 5 |
| Pro | **$199** | Small design/build firm; 3 users | 15 |
| Studio | **$399** | Busy sales and pre-construction team; 10 users | 40 |

Every tier includes the core concept → scope → revision → bid-comparison/export workflow. A package means one property, three initial concepts, one selected project, up to three revisions and one three-bid comparison. Viewing and exporting existing projects does not consume another package. This is the proposed commercial meter; it is not enforced by the local prototype. Keep quotas visible, stop for an explicit upgrade when exhausted, and never silently charge overages. Hold off on unlimited generation and annual discounts until compute, support and renewal economics are measured.

Run the first five four-week pilots at the **$99 Solo price**, including one onboarding session and review of the first package. Collect the customer's own cost catalog and measure repeated use. Do not bundle ongoing human design work into software pricing: quote optional services separately, disclose what professionals must verify, and exclude one-time fees from MRR. A sample demo can be free without creating a permanent unlimited free tier.

Illustrative buyer ROI, to validate: four staff hours saved/month × $75 loaded hourly cost = $300, against the $199 Pro plan. Break-even on that assumption is about 2.7 hours saved per month, without assuming any extra jobs won. Measure actual proposal time and correction effort; the savings and hourly rate are hypotheses, not promises.

## $100K MRR backward plan

**Competitive pricing requires roughly 400 customers, not 50.** One exact mix is **100 Solo × $99 + 150 Pro × $199 + 152 Studio × $399 = $100,398 MRR across 402 retained firms** by December 31, 2026. At Pro pricing alone it takes 503 firms; at Studio pricing alone, 251. Count collected recurring subscription revenue net of discounts; separate tax, services and any channel revenue share. The example below assumes full-price direct subscriptions and no channel commission. A 20% commission on every subscription would reduce $100,398 to $80,318.40, so a paid channel needs a larger customer count or different economics.

This is a conditional stretch target from a September prototype. A smaller operating case is 100 customers at approximately $249 blended monthly revenue, or $24,900 MRR; validate retention before treating either case as predictable. The December stretch depends on a channel that can activate hundreds of firms, alongside a low-touch buying and onboarding flow. Founder sales alone are unlikely to support it in fourteen weeks.

| Date | Target, not forecast | Required evidence / decision |
|---|---|---|
| September 11–20 | 20 interviews; observe 5 actual quoting workflows; recruit 5 pilots at $99 | Obtain time/cost baselines; confirm the buyer and existing software |
| September 30 | 5 paying pilots; $495 MRR if recurring and collected | Weekly use, reliable scope and explicit willingness to renew |
| October 31 | Stretch: 40 firms, approximately $10K MRR | Two credible case studies, onboarding under 30 minutes, one channel activation trial |
| November 30 | Stretch: 150 firms, approximately $37K MRR | A second repeatable channel; measured activation, renewal and service cost |
| December 31 | Stretch: 402 retained firms at the exact tier mix above; $100,398 MRR | Sustainable retention and support; sufficient partner-sourced activation |

Acquisition burden without a channel: 402 customers at an assumed 25% demo-to-paid conversion need 1,608 qualified demos. At 10% targeted-contact-to-demo conversion, that means 16,080 relevant contacts, roughly 1,150 per week over 14 weeks. This is a reason to test distribution, not an instruction to mass-message contractors. No outreach or partnership has been executed by this prototype.

Concrete channel hypothesis: two distributors, dealer networks or associations each activate 150 retained firms, plus 102 direct customers. At assumed 20% activation and 80% retention through December, the partners need a combined reachable base of roughly 1,875 relevant firms. The direct contribution needs about 408 qualified demos at the assumed 25% conversion. Partner reach is not committed distribution; obtain an actual co-marketing/activation trial and measure resulting paid users. If no channel produces at least 20 paying activations by October 31, revise the December revenue expectation rather than inflate prices to repair the spreadsheet.

At the target mix, full included usage is **8,830 packages/month** (500 Solo + 2,250 Pro + 6,080 Studio). At an illustrative all-in variable cost of $3/package, monthly package cost is $26,490, leaving about 74% before other variable support/payment/channel costs. At $5/package that falls to about 56%. These are sensitivity inputs, not measured costs. Even five minutes human review per package would consume about 736 hours/month at full usage. A software-priced offer cannot depend on universal concierge review. Measure API/image/render cost, failed-call retries and support from the first pilot; reduce included volume or optimize delivery if a contribution-margin target above 70% cannot be sustained. Keep professional review with the contractor or price it explicitly as a separate service.

## Distribution and commercial models

| Model | Near-term view |
|---|---|
| Contractor SaaS subscription | Primary experiment: recurring, measurable workflow with an identifiable payer |
| Design / pre-construction fee | Useful pilot/revenue bridge; often one-off, not MRR |
| Homeowner transaction fee | Later; requires repeatable distribution and contractor supply |
| Contractor success fee | Attribution/disintermediation risk and ranking conflict; do not start here |
| Financing referral | Later only after consent, proper partners, and a real transaction workflow |
| Materials procurement | Later after reliable quantities, supplier coverage, and landed-cost handling |
| B2B2C distributor / retailer | Promising channel experiment after 3–5 successful contractor pilots |
| Property manager / enterprise | Repeated work but procurement/integration cycles may miss the December goal |

Use personal introductions, local trade events, outdoor-kitchen/paver distributors, and contractor referrals for discovery. Start with exported scopes that complement existing estimating software; integrate only when pilots repeatedly demand it.

## Product and data advantage

The durable asset could be a permissioned record linking homeowner intent, supplied evidence, assumptions, design versions, scope IDs, quoted omissions, accepted selections, change orders and actual outcomes. Consistent IDs make corrections and cost learning reusable across the workflow. Regional cost knowledge and professional-reviewed rules may become valuable with enough verified data. Neither a moat nor a proprietary dataset exists today. Obtain customer consent, minimize private property data, and isolate customer-specific pricing.

## First post-hackathon build sequence

1. Import a real contractor cost catalog and mark its date and region. Keep synthetic estimates out of real proposals.
2. Add site-survey/manual-rule ingestion with source evidence and human approval.
3. Add durable project storage, accounts and authorization only for real multi-user pilots.
4. Export proposals into existing tools; measure turnaround, scope correction rate, close rate and gross profit.
5. Add change-order comparison only after accepted scope becomes a real recurring record.

Go/no-go after 30 days: at least three firms pay to continue, use it weekly on multiple leads, and can describe a measured benefit. If firms only want attractive pictures, demand extensive unpaid design services, or reject meaningful subscription pricing, revise the offer before hiring or scaling acquisition. The December target is a forcing function, not a reason to misstate demand.
